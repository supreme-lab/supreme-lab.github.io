AI-in-the-Loop

Privacy Preserving Real-Time Scam Detection Leveraging LLMs and Federated Learning

Authors: Anon. — Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies

Abstract

Scams exploiting real-time social engineering—such as phishing, impersonation, and phone fraud—are a persistent and evolving threat across digital platforms. Existing detection systems are typically reactive, offering limited protection during active scammer interactions. We propose a novel, privacy-preserving, AI-in-the-loop framework that proactively detects and disrupts scam conversations in real time. Our system integrates instruction-tuned large language models (LLMs) with a privacy-aware utility function that selects responses balancing conversational engagement and harm minimization. To prevent disclosure of personal or sensitive information, we apply rigorous PII scoring and enforce hard safety thresholds. Crucially, we couple this mechanism with federated learning, enabling on-device model updates without raw data aggregation—preserving user privacy while supporting continual learning from diverse, real-world interactions. Experimental results show that our approach improves scammer deception, sustains engagement, and adapts dynamically across scenarios. This is the first system to combine real-time conversational scam-baiting with privacy-preserving federated learning, offering a new paradigm for proactive, adaptive, and trustworthy scam defense.

Introduction

Scams in online social networks exploit users’ digital footprints, emotional states, and transactional interests. Traditional detection pipelines often lag behind evolving scam tactics. Our work introduces AI-in-the-Loop, where large language models (LLMs) participate in both detection and response generation, supported by federated learning to preserve user data privacy:contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}.

User Survey & Ethics

We conducted a survey with 148 diverse participants across Bangladesh, India, and Mexico. Key findings:

  • 74% expressed strong support for proactive AI-based scam defense.
  • 79% agreed such systems could help them avoid scams altogether.
  • Data was collected anonymously without personal identifiers; only optional demographics were used.

This survey shaped both the response generation module and the scam scenarios used in evaluation:contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}.

System Design

Our framework consists of four interconnected components:

  1. Real-time scam detection (detects malicious content as it occurs).
  2. AI-based scambaiting responses (to delay, confuse, or neutralize scammers).
  3. Safety-aware utility evaluation (balances engagement vs. harm penalties).
  4. Federated learning (privacy-preserving continual model adaptation).

Threat models addressed include gradient leakage (DLG, iDLG), mitigated using Differential Privacy (DP-SGD), ensuring resilience against inference attacks:contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}.

Methodology

We evaluate engagement (E) vs. harm (H) with a utility function parameterized by reward (α) and penalty (γ). Grid search across 12 configurations shows that α = 2.0, γ = 1.0 yields optimal trade-offs for real-world deployment:contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}.

Features include semantic similarity (BERTScore), risk assessment, and multi-turn conversation analysis. Federated optimization aggregates local updates while respecting non-IID client heterogeneity:contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}.

Results

  • Model Performance: Safety-aligned LLMs (LlamaGuard, MD-Judge) show high scam detection rates, with unsafe responses scoring ~0.8–1.0 vs. 0.1 for safe:contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}.
  • Human Evaluation: Fine-tuned MD-Judge outperformed LlamaGuard3 across Realism, Engagement, Safety, and Effectiveness, with statistical significance:contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}.
  • Utility Distribution: Balanced trade-off between engagement and harm ensures safe but natural AI responses:contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}.

Conclusion

AI-in-the-Loop demonstrates that integrating detection, response, and federated adaptation provides an effective and privacy-preserving solution to real-time scam defense. The framework balances safety with user engagement and generalizes across diverse populations.
