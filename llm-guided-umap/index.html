ABSTRACT

We present a novel approach that uses large language models (LLMs) to assist graph machine learning (GML) under personalization and privacy constraints. Our approach integrates (i) LLM-assisted data augmentation for sparse graphs, (ii) prompt and instruction tuning to adapt foundation models to graph tasks, and (iii) in-context learning to provide few-shot graph reasoning signals. These signals parameterize a Dynamic-UMAP manifold of client-specific graph embeddings inside a Bayesian variational objective for personalized federated learning. The method supports node classification and link prediction in low-resource settings and aligns LLM latent representations with graph structure via a cross-modal regularizer. We provide a convergence sketch for our variational aggregation, detail a privacy threat model with moments-accountant DP, and report applications to knowledge graph completion, recommendation-style link prediction, and citation/product graphs, along with proposed evaluation protocols for LLM-augmented GML.

1 Introduction

Graphs power applications from recommendation and scientific discovery to knowledge graph (KG) completion and question answering (QA) [10, 12]. In practice, graphs are heterogeneous, private, and sparse across clients (e.g., institutions or edge devices). Meanwhile, LLMs encode extensive world knowledge and can be prompted or instruction-tuned to provide structural priors, textual context, and few-shot signals [5]. We ask: How can LLMs assist personalized federated GML when labeled edges/nodes are scarce and data cannot leave clients?

We propose LLM-Guided Dynamic-UMAP (LG-DUMAP), a Gaussian variational framework that: (1) builds client-specific graph manifolds using Dynamic-UMAP over graph embeddings; (2) injects LLM guidance via prompt/instruction-tuned text encoders and in-context exemplars; (3) performs personalized federated variational inference with privacy-preserving aggregation of similarity prototypes (markers) summarizing local similarity structures; and (4) aligns LLM and graph latent spaces to improve low-resource performance.

Contributions.

  • A parametric UMAP-style manifold objective coupled with variational marker aggregation for personalized federation.
  • Cross-modal alignment to LLM text space and calibrated pseudo-edges with abstention for robustness.
  • A concrete privacy threat model with secure aggregation, per-client clipping, and moments-accountant DP; and an attack-driven evaluation via membership inference.
  • Expanded baselines (Per-FedAvg, pFedMe, Ditto, FedProx; LightGCN/RotatE/ComplEx/CompGCN) with systems evidence (cost/latency, partial participation) and worst-client/percentile reporting.

2 Related Work

2.1 LLM-Augmented Graph Learning

Hybrid pipelines use language encoders to guide graph learning by supplying semantic priors, pseudo-labels, and retrieval-style context [5]. In text-rich graphs (e.g., citation networks, product catalogs, and KGs), LLM embeddings can act as additional node features, enable label/edge proposals under extreme sparsity, and furnish rationales. We leverage these capabilities with a calibrated admission policy for pseudo-edges (temperature scaling, confidence thresholding) to mitigate overconfident hallucinations [9]. Compared to text-only or late-fusion baselines [10, 12], we integrate language supervision within a geometric objective, coupling cross-modal alignment with a parametric manifold learner.

2.2 Personalized Federated Learning for (Graph) Models

FedAvg assumes homogeneous objectives and suffers on non-IID clients; remedies include FedProx [15] and personalization strategies such as Per-FedAvg [7], pFedMe [6], and Ditto [14]. Most operate at the level of model parameters. In contrast, we personalize through a lightweight variational prototype layer (markers) that summarizes local similarity structure and is safe to share under privacy budgets. This differs from prior federated GNN work that aggregates entire GNN weights/gradients, incurring larger communication and privacy footprints. We also incorporate partial participation and report worst-client/percentile metrics to characterize non-IID regimes.

2.3 Parametric Manifold Learning and UMAP

UMAP is a widely used non-linear dimensionality reduction technique built on fuzzy simplicial sets [16]; parametric variants learn an explicit mapping that can be amortized across data and clients [18]. Unlike purely unsupervised application of (parametric) UMAP to graph embeddings, our Dynamic-UMAP is optimized jointly with downstream tasks and a cross-modal regularizer tying the manifold to LLM embeddings. We additionally introduce markers—a KDE-style similarity model on interpoint distances in the learned space—which (i) acts as a compact, aggregatable representation for federation and (ii) improves link modeling in low-resource regimes. We report manifold trustworthiness/continuity and representation-similarity diagnostics (CKA, Procrustes) to probe geometry and modality alignment [8, 13].

2.4 Knowledge-Graph and Recommendation Baselines

For KG completion, translational and rotational families (TransE/RotatE) and bilinear approaches (ComplEx) remain strong baselines [3, 20, 21]. Structure-aware GNNs such as CompGCN add relation composition into message passing [22]. In recommendation, LightGCN shows that stripped-down propagation without non-linearities is competitive on collaborative filtering [11]. Our evaluation situates LG-DUMAP against these methods and emphasizes few-shot/cold-start conditions where text priors help most. We also include heterophily stress tests (e.g., Chameleon, Squirrel) where neighborhood homophily is weak and geometric signals matter [17].

3 Methodology

Each client \(k \in \{1, \ldots, N\}\) owns a private graph \(G_k = (V_k, E_k, X_k, T_k)\) with node features \(X_k\), optional node/edge texts \(T_k\), and tasks: node classification or link prediction. Clients cannot share raw data. The server maintains global priors but aggregates only privacy-preserving statistics.

LLM guidance. An LLM \(\mathcal{L}\) provides (a) text embeddings for \(T_k\), (b) instruction/prompt-tuned decoding to propose edges or node labels in low-resource regimes, and (c) in-context exemplars for few-shot behaviors. We denote the LLM text encoder by \(h_{\theta}(\cdot)\) and a lightweight adapter/prompt \(P\).

We integrate UMAP, graph encoders, and LLM guidance within a variational federated objective.

3.1 Graph and Text Fusion Embeddings

We compute node embeddings by fusing a GNN encoder \(\phi_k\) with LLM text embeddings:

\[ \tilde{X}_k = \mathrm{Fuse}\big(\mathrm{GNN}_{\phi_k}(G_k),\; h_{\theta}(T_k; P)\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{|V_k| \times m}. \tag{1} \]

3.2 Dynamic-UMAP Manifold

We learn a low-dimensional manifold preserving local graph structure and LLM priors [16, 18]:

\[ Z_k = g_{\beta_k}(\tilde{X}_k) \in \mathbb{R}^{|V_k| \times d}. \tag{2} \]

The UMAP objective uses neighborhood probabilities \((p_{ij}, q_{ij})\) with

\[ \mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{\mathrm{UMAP}} = -\sum_{(i,j)\in E^{\mathrm{nn}}_k}\Big[p_{ij}\log q_{ij} + (1-p_{ij})\log(1-q_{ij})\Big], \tag{3} \] \[ q_{ij} = \Big(1 + a\lVert z_i - z_j \rVert^{2b}\Big)^{-1}, \tag{4} \]

where \((a, b)\) follow standard UMAP settings [16]. We optimize \(g_{\beta_k}\) jointly with downstream losses.

3.3 LLM-Augmented Similarity and Markers

Let \(s_{ij} = \lVert z_i - z_j \rVert_2\) in \(Z_k\). Markers are scalar prototypes in the distance space (not vectors in \(Z_k\)). We model a soft assignment of pairwise distances to \(\mathcal{M} = \{m_1, \ldots, m_{|\mathcal{M}|}\} \subset \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\) via a KDE-style mixture:

\[ p^e_{ij} = \frac{ \exp\Big(-\lVert s_{ij} - m_e \rVert^2/(2\sigma^2_{\mathrm{kern}})\Big) }{ \sum_{m'\in\mathcal{M}} \exp\Big(-\lVert s_{ij} - m' \rVert^2/(2\sigma^2_{\mathrm{kern}})\Big) }. \tag{5} \]

Markers are initialized from labeled edges and calibrated LLM pseudo-edges (accepted if confidence \(c\ge\tau\) after temperature scaling).

3.4 Cross-Modal Alignment

We align \(Z_k\) with LLM embeddings using cosine similarity with explicit normalization:

\[ \mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{\mathrm{align}} = \sum_{i\in V_k}\left(1 - \frac{\langle z_i,\; h_{\theta}(T_{k,i}; P)\rangle}{\lVert z_i\rVert_2\, \lVert h_{\theta}(T_{k,i}; P)\rVert_2} \right). \tag{6} \]

We assess representation similarity with CKA and Procrustes analyses [8, 13].

3.5 Client Variational Objective

For client \(k\), the loss is

\[ \mathcal{L}_k = \mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi_k}(\mathcal{M})}\big[\mathcal{J}_k(Z_k)\big] + \lambda\,\mathrm{KL}\big(q_{\phi_k}(\mathcal{M})\,\Vert\, s(\mathcal{M})\big) + \gamma\,\mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{\mathrm{align}} + \eta\,\mathcal{L}^{(k)}_{\mathrm{UMAP}}. \tag{7} \]

where \(\mathcal{J}_k\) includes classification NLL and link prediction BCE using \(p^e_{ij}\). The prior \(s(\mathcal{M})\) is a simple Gaussian mixture over marker locations; \(q_{\phi_k}(\mathcal{M})\) is the client’s variational posterior over markers.

3.6 Server Aggregation (Personalized)

The server aggregates markers only:

\[ M^{t+1} = M^{t} + \frac{\sum_k w_k\,\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi_k}(\mathcal{M})}\big[M_k - M^{t}\big]}{\sum_k w_k}. \tag{8} \]

No raw data, node embeddings, or GNN weights are shared.

Key Hyperparameters

UMAP dim \(d=32\), neighbors = 15; markers: \(|\mathcal{M}^{+}|=8\), \(|\mathcal{M}^{-}|=8\), bandwidth \(\sigma_{\mathrm{kern}}=1.0\); alignment \(\gamma=0.2\), UMAP \(\eta=1.0\), KL \(\lambda=0.1\); Adam lr \(2\times 10^{-3}\) (GNN/UMAP) and \(5\times 10^{-5}\) (adapters); rounds 50, local epochs 2, batch 1024 node-pairs; DP: clip \(C=1.0\), Gaussian noise scale \(\sigma_{\mathrm{dp}}\) as in Table 2; acceptance threshold \(\tau\in\{0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9\}\).

4 Threat Model and Differential Privacy Guarantees

We assume an honest-but-curious server. Clients never disclose raw graphs/texts. Communication uses secure aggregation [2]. We apply per-client clipping at norm \(C\) and add Gaussian noise with variance \(\sigma^2_{dp}C^2\) to shared marker statistics before aggregation. Using the moments accountant with sampling rate \(q\) and \(T\) rounds, we report overall \((\epsilon,\delta)\) [1] and evaluate empirical privacy via membership inference (Section 5).

5 Calibration and Safety for LLM Proposals

We calibrate LLM pseudo-edges via temperature scaling (\(T\)) on a validation slice and report ECE (15 bins) and Brier scores [4, 9]. Pseudo-edges with confidence \(\hat{p}\ge\tau\) are admitted; we study F1 vs. \(\tau\in\{0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9\}\) and acceptance counts.

Calibration Metrics

Expected Calibration Error (ECE): \(\mathrm{ECE} = \sum_b \frac{|B_b|}{n}\,|\mathrm{acc}(B_b)-\mathrm{conf}(B_b)|\). Brier: \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_i(\hat{p}_i-y_i)^2\).

6 Theory

Assume each client loss \(\mathcal{L}_k\) is \(L\)-smooth and the update variance is bounded. The aggregated marker update is a stochastic approximation to a stationary point of \(\mathcal{L}(M)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_k \mathcal{L}_k(M)\).

Proposition 1 (Convergence of Marker Averaging). Under \(L\)-smoothness, bounded variance, and diminishing step size (or Polyak averaging) with partial participation rate \(p>0\), we have \(\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}(M^t)\rVert = 0\), with an \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{t})\) rate under standard assumptions.

Lemma 1 (Alignment improves local curvature). If LLM embeddings form \(\delta\)-separated clusters consistent with labels and \(\gamma>0\), the alignment term adds a positive semidefinite component to the neighborhood Hessian, reducing variance of \(p^e_{ij}\) and accelerating convergence.

7 Experiments

We evaluate node classification and link prediction under federated, low-resource settings with textual side information [12].

7.1 Datasets, Splits, and Evaluation Protocol

Datasets. Cora, Citeseer (node classification); ogbn-arxiv and ogbn-products (node classification); FB15k-237 (KG completion); and Chameleon/Squirrel for heterophily stress [12, 17].

Federated splits. We form \(N=20\) non-IID clients via topic/label stratification. Few-shot regimes use 5–40 labeled nodes/edges per client; cold-start introduces 30% text-only nodes. Clients participate with sampling \(q\in\{0.2,0.5,1.0\}\) per round (partial participation). Text-only nodes contribute features via \(h_{\theta}(\cdot)\) and enter kNN construction on \(\tilde{X}_k\); their labels/edges are proposed by calibrated LLMs.

Metrics. Accuracy / Micro-F1 (with worst-client and 10th-percentile reporting), KG MRR/Hits@K, manifold trustworthiness/continuity, and cross-modal alignment (cosine, CKA, Procrustes) [8, 13]. Privacy is summarized by \((\epsilon,\delta)\) and empirical attack AUROC for membership inference [19]. We also track systems cost: KB/round, time/round, LLM tokens, adapter size.

7.2 Baselines and Implementation Details

Baselines. Federated personalization: Local-GNN, FedAvg-GNN, Per-FedAvg [7], pFedMe [6], Ditto [14], FedProx [15]. KG/link: TransE [3], RotatE [20], ComplEx [21], CompGCN [22]. Recommendation: LightGCN [11]. Fusion: LLM Text-only (frozen encoder) [5], and LLM+GNN (late fusion) [10, 12].

Our variants. LG-DUMAP(P) uses prompt-tuned adapters; LG-DUMAP(I) uses instruction-tuned adapters with the same backbone.

Setup. GraphSAGE (2-layer, hidden=256) [10]; Dynamic-UMAP with \(d=32\), neighbors = 15; markers \(|\mathcal{M}^{+}|=8\), \(|\mathcal{M}^{-}|=8\), bandwidth \(\sigma_{\mathrm{kern}}=1.0\); Adam lr \(2\times 10^{-3}\) (GNN/UMAP), \(5\times 10^{-5}\) (adapters); 50 rounds; 2 local epochs; batch size 1024 node-pairs; \(\gamma=0.2\), \(\eta=1.0\), \(\lambda=0.1\); frozen text encoder with 16 prompt tokens.

7.3 Results and Analysis

Main results. LG-DUMAP consistently outperforms both graph-only and naive language–graph fusion baselines across citation, product, and KG tasks.

Observations. (1) Graph-only vs. federation. FedAvg-GNN improves over Local-GNN on all datasets (e.g., Cora: +2.3 Acc; ogbn-arxiv: +2.2 F1). (2) Language alone is insufficient. LLM Text-only underperforms graph-aware models on structural tasks, indicating topology remains essential. (3) Naive fusion helps, but falls short. Late fusion adds text features post hoc and yields steady gains over graph-only baselines, but lacks manifold-level coupling. (4) Our approach closes the gap. LG-DUMAP(P) delivers additional gains via prompt-tuned guidance and Dynamic-UMAP. (5) Instruction-tuned variant is best. LG-DUMAP(I) consistently wins (e.g., ogbn-arxiv: 74.3 F1). The uplift over late fusion supports cross-modal alignment and marker-based similarity modeling rather than feature concatenation.

Few-shot, cold-start, and alignment. In low-label regimes and 30% cold-start, LG-DUMAP(I) retains the largest fraction of full-data performance; manifold trustworthiness/continuity and CKA/Procrustes indicate tighter coupling between \(Z\) and \(h_{\theta}\) than late fusion.

Efficiency and privacy trade-offs. Table 4 summarizes communication/runtime and DP effects. Adding calibrated language guidance and parametric UMAP raises cost modestly but improves accuracy/F1. Client-level DP (Gaussian mechanism with moments accountant) degrades F1 gracefully as \(\epsilon\) tightens, while still outperforming the graph-only federation baseline at moderate budgets.

A Convergence Sketch (Extended)

Let \(\mathcal{L}(M)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_k \mathcal{L}_k(M)\) be \(L\)-smooth and let the aggregated update be \(M^{t+1}=M^t + \frac{1}{N}\sum_k \mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi_k}}[M_k - M^t]\) with bounded variance. With client sampling rate \(p>0\) and diminishing step size or Polyak averaging, standard stochastic approximation results yield \(\lim_t \lVert \nabla \mathcal{L}(M^t)\rVert \to 0\) at \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{t})\). The KL term stabilizes around Gaussian mixture priors; alignment adds local positive curvature when semantic clusters are separable.
